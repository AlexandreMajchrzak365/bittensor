Step 3

The ecosystem's economy

Where do TAO come from, where do they go, and what gives them value? Three simulators to see it.

21 million, and not one more

Like Bitcoin, Bittensor will never create more than 21 million TAO. At first, the network created 1 per block, or 7,200 a day. Each time half of what is left to create has been issued, creation is cut in half: this is the halving. The first one happened in December 2025, at 10.5 million TAO.

About 11.7 million TAO in circulation in November 2026.

Each subnet has its currency: alpha

Since February 2025, each subnet has a pool holding TAO on one side and its own currency, alpha, on the other. To support a subnet, you pour TAO into the pool and get alpha in return. The price depends on the ratio between the two: the more you buy, the more expensive alpha gets. This extra cost is called slippage.

Who gets what in a subnet?

new
alpha

At each round, the alpha created in a subnet is shared out. By default:

  • 41% to the miners, according to their scores
  • 41% to the validators and the stakers backing them
  • 18% to the subnet owner

Since October 2026 (eTAO), each owner can change this split: at most 33% for themselves, at most 50% for validators, and at least 33% for miners.

The virtuous circle… in theory

  1. Customers pay for a service (an AI API, a rented graphics card…)
  2. The subnet uses that revenue to buy back its alpha
  3. The alpha price rises and attracts TAO
  4. More incoming TAO, so more emissions for the subnet
  5. Miners are paid better and the service improves

This circle only holds if the revenue is real. In October 2026, subnets together earned about 50 million dollars a year according to a community estimate, rising fast but still well below the value of the TAO handed out. Much of the economy therefore still rests on speculation.

Two ways to stake

On subnet 0 (“root”)

Your TAO stay TAO. The risk is lower, and so is the return. Most guides recommend it to beginners.

On a subnet

Your TAO are swapped for the subnet's alpha. If the subnet succeeds, alpha gains value; if it fails or is deregistered, you can lose a large part of your stake.

The risks, plainly

  • Volatility. TAO has lost more than two thirds of its value since its record. Alpha currencies move even more.
  • Governance. In April 2026, the Covenant AI team left Bittensor with its three subnets (including Templar), accusing co-founder Jacob Steeves of keeping control of the network, which he disputed. TAO lost about 15% in two hours. In June 2026, the foundation published a plan to fully decentralise the network by December 2027.
  • Deregistration. A subnet with little support can lose its slot, and its alpha with it.
  • Exchange-traded funds. Managers such as Grayscale have applied to launch exchange-traded funds on TAO. An application is not an approval.
